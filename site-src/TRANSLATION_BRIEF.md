# Translation brief: meding.dev

## What this is
meding.dev is the personal reference website of Michael Meding, a German mining executive who is Managing Director of McEwen Copper and leads the Los Azules copper project in San Juan, Argentina. The site is written in the third person, like a well-sourced encyclopedia entry. Its main readers are search engines and AI assistants, then investors, journalists, event organisers and business partners. Every sentence must be accurate, idiomatic and read as if written by a senior native-speaking financial or mining journalist. Do not produce translationese.

Source file: `content/en.json` (English is the master). Write your output to `content/<lang>.json`.

## Hard rules
1. The output JSON must have exactly the same structure and keys as `en.json`, in the same order, with the same list lengths. Keys never change.
2. Translate every string value, except:
   - keys whose name starts with `_`, and the whole `quotes_reference_do_not_translate` list (copy unchanged);
   - fields named `url`, `r`, `y`, `m`, `mid`, `target`, `card_born_r` (copy unchanged);
   - `ui.lang_tag_*` values (copy unchanged).
3. Keep every citation token `[[r:...]]` exactly as written, attached at the end of the same sentence or item. Never translate or reorder the keys inside it. In CJK text place the token after the full stop 。 exactly as in English (after the final punctuation).
4. Proper names stay in their official form: McEwen Copper, McEwen Inc., Andes Corporación Minera S.A., GEMERA, CAEM, Cámara Minera de San Juan, Argentina Cobre, Panorama Minero, Stellantis, Nuton, Rio Tinto, IFC, Société Générale, Sprott Natural Resource Investment Partners, Barrick Gold, Veladero, Shandong Gold, Trafigura, PwC, Novartis, YPF Luz, universities, awards' Spanish names (Premio INVERTIR, Diploma Migrante destacado), newspapers, laws and resolution numbers (Resolución 1553/2025, Law 27,742). In Japanese and Chinese, give an established local rendering followed by the original in parentheses at the first mention within each longer text block where it helps search (for example the person's name, McEwen Copper, Los Azules, San Juan, Calingasta, RIGI). Search the web to find renderings actually used by Japanese or Chinese mining and financial media (JOGMEC, Nikkei, 中国矿业报, 上海有色网, 财新, Mining.com Chinese coverage). If none exists, transliterate following standard conventions and keep the Latin form in parentheses.
5. Quoted words in Spanish inside English strings (for example “Todos mis geólogos …” followed by an English gloss in parentheses): keep the original Spanish quotation and translate only the gloss into the target language. In the Spanish translation drop the gloss.
6. Numbers: never change a figure. Convert only the formatting to the target locale's conventions (decimal and thousands separators, currency position, large-number words such as Mrd., 億, 亿). Be consistent throughout the file. Units: metres, tonnes, pounds (lb) of copper, US$.
7. Mining and securities terms must match the standard terminology used in that language under CRIM/CIM, JORC or CRIRSCO-family codes: proven and probable mineral reserves; measured, indicated and inferred mineral resources; feasibility study; preliminary economic assessment; qualified person; net present value (NPV8); internal rate of return; payback; C1 cash cost; AISC; heap leach; SX-EW (solvent extraction and electrowinning); copper cathode; porphyry; supergene; tailings dam; open pit; final investment decision; senior secured term loan; share purchase warrants. Verify the key terms by web search before using them.
8. The cautionary text in `la.tech_p1` to `la.tech_p4` is regulatory in nature. Translate it faithfully and completely; do not soften or shorten it. Fill `la.tech_translation_note` (empty in English) with one sentence saying that this translation is provided for convenience and that the English version of the technical and cautionary information prevails.
9. `la.loan_quote` is Michael Meding's verbatim English statement. Leave `la.loan_quote` unchanged (English original). Put your translation of it in `la.loan_quote_tr`, and in `la.loan_quote_note` write a short label meaning "Translation of the original English statement".
10. `quotes_tr` is a list of 16 translations aligned with `quotes_reference_do_not_translate`. For each item, translate the original quote into the target language. If the original quote is already in the target language, use an empty string "". Translate the meaning faithfully and naturally; do not polish the speaker's words beyond what the original says.
11. Meta titles and descriptions are for search results: keep titles under about 60 Latin characters (Japanese and Chinese under about 32 characters) and descriptions under about 155 Latin characters (Japanese and Chinese under about 80 characters). Put the most important words first: Michael Meding, Los Azules, McEwen Copper, copper, Argentina.
12. Style: plain, precise, confident, factual. Short and medium sentences. No marketing superlatives beyond what the English says. No em dashes or spaced dashes as sentence punctuation; use commas, colons, semicolons or full stops (ranges like 2022–2030 are fine). Do not add or drop facts.
13. `meta.lang_name` is the language's own name as shown in a language menu. `meta.og_locale` is the Open Graph locale for the target market.
14. `speaking.cities`: translate the values (the keys are English and must stay). `speaking.roles`: translate each value; keep event titles in quotation marks in their original language and translate the gloss in parentheses, as in rule 5.
15. After writing, re-read your whole file once against the English, fix any omission, mistranslation or inconsistency, and validate that the file parses as JSON (for example with `python3 -c "import json;json.load(open('content/<lang>.json'))"`).

## Deliverable
Write the file, validate it, then reply with: (a) a list of the terminology choices you made for the key terms in rule 7 and for the names in rule 4, each with the source you checked; (b) anything in the English you found ambiguous or possibly wrong.
