Los Azules copper project

Los Azules is a copper porphyry in the Andes of Calingasta, San Juan, Argentina, owned by McEwen Copper and led by Michael Meding. The October 2025 feasibility study designs an open pit, heap leach and SX-EW mine that would make 99.99% copper cathode at site, with no tailings dam and 100% renewable power. It was the first copper project admitted to Argentina's RIGI. The final investment decision is expected in mid-2027 and commercial cathode production is targeted for 2030.[1–5]

A 21-year mine plan, with room to grow

The feasibility study base case is a 21-year mine life based on proven and probable reserves. McEwen Copper has also identified growth opportunities outside the feasibility study. Rio Tinto's Nuton leaching technology could allow the primary sulphides to be processed with the existing infrastructure, or a conventional concentrator could give higher copper recoveries and also recover gold and silver. McEwen Copper has said either route could extend mine life by 30 years or more. Neither opportunity is included in the feasibility study base case.[1]

This potential extension comes from strategic planning work that considers inferred mineral resources. Inferred mineral resources are too speculative geologically to have economic considerations applied to them, and there is no certainty they will ever be converted to mineral reserves. No commercial agreement with Nuton is yet in place. A concentrator, unlike the base-case heap leach design, would require tailings storage and use more water. Either route would need further studies, permits and financing. Technical information and qualified persons ↓

Argentina and copper

  • Argentina has not produced copper at scale since Bajo de la Alumbrera closed in 2018.[19]
  • A national Mining Secretariat report projects that output could exceed 1.5 million tonnes a year by 2035.[20]
  • RIGI (Law 27,742) offers a 30-year stability guarantee on its incentives for investments above US$200 million.[21]
  • S&P Global projects copper demand of 42 million tonnes by 2040, a 50% increase from current levels.[22]
  • The IEA expects copper to record the largest volume growth in demand, adding about 7 million tonnes to 2040.[23]

Questions and answers

What is Los Azules?

A copper porphyry project in the Andes of Calingasta, San Juan, owned by McEwen Copper. Its October 2025 feasibility study plans an open pit, heap leach and SX-EW operation producing 99.99% copper cathode at site, averaging 204,800 tonnes a year in its first five years.[1]

Where exactly is Los Azules?

In the department of Calingasta, San Juan province, at about 3,500 metres, roughly 80 km west-northwest of Calingasta and 6 km east of the Chilean border.[6]

Who leads Los Azules?

Michael Meding, Managing Director of McEwen Copper, has led the project since February 2022.[2, 8]

Who owns McEwen Copper?

McEwen Inc. 46.3%, Stellantis 18.2%, Nuton (a Rio Tinto venture) 17.2%, Rob McEwen 13.0%, Victor Smorgon Group 3.0% and other shareholders 2.3%.[6]

When will Los Azules produce copper?

McEwen Copper expects a final investment decision and full project financing in mid-2027 and is targeting commercial copper cathode production in 2030. These are forward-looking targets that depend on financing, permits and other risks.[5]

Why is the Los Azules design different?

The design would make finished copper cathode at the mine instead of concentrate, with no tailings dam, 100% renewable power, 74% less water than conventional milling and a carbon intensity of 1,082 kg CO2-e per tonne against an estimated industry average of 4,026.[1, 3]

Is Los Azules approved under Argentina's RIGI?

Yes. Resolución 1553/2025, published on 14 October 2025, admitted Los Azules with an investment plan of US$2,672 million. It was the first copper project approved under the regime.[4, 11]

How large is Los Azules?

The feasibility study reports proven and probable reserves containing 10.2 billion pounds of copper (1,023 million tonnes at 0.453% Cu), effective 3 September 2025. Separately, and exclusive of those reserves, it reports measured and indicated resources containing 5.4 billion pounds and inferred resources containing 20.0 billion pounds. Mineral resources that are not mineral reserves do not have demonstrated economic viability, inferred resources carry high geological uncertainty, and the categories must not be added together.[1]

How long could Los Azules operate?

The feasibility study base case is a 21-year mine life based on proven and probable reserves. Outside the study, McEwen Copper has identified that processing the primary sulphides with Rio Tinto's Nuton technology or a conventional concentrator could extend mine life by 30 years or more. That potential is not part of the base case, relies in part on inferred mineral resources, and would need further studies, permits and financing.[1]

What is the US$240 million term loan?

A four-year senior secured loan at 12.0% a year that McEwen Copper closed on 27 August 2026, with Sprott Natural Resource Investment Partners (US$112 million), Rob McEwen (US$85 million) and other lenders (US$43 million). It funds engineering and early works toward the final investment decision.[5]

How is the project being financed?

McEwen Copper has raised US$453 million in private placements since 2021, including from Stellantis and Nuton, and closed a US$240 million term loan in August 2026. It has appointed Société Générale as sole financial advisor for project debt financing and signed a collaboration agreement with the IFC to align the project with IFC standards for potential future financing. McEwen Copper has said it is seeking an overall financing package of about US$4 billion, which is not yet committed.[5, 6, 15–17]
